Introduction

Welcome to your comprehensive guide to traveling Japan! This handbook contains a carefully planned 7-day itinerary through Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka, essential Japanese phrases, local travel tips, and an interactive map to enhance your journey.

Japan offers a perfect blend of ancient traditions and cutting-edge modernity. From serene temples and gardens to bustling markets and high-tech districts, this journey will immerse you in the rich culture and stunning landscapes of Japan.

7-Day Japan Itinerary

Day 1: Tokyo - Arrival and Exploration

  • Morning: Arrive at Narita/Haneda Airport, transfer to hotel
  • Afternoon: Explore Asakusa district and visit Senso-ji Temple
  • Evening: Tokyo Skytree for panoramic night views

Day 2: Tokyo - Modern Culture

  • Morning: Shibuya Crossing and Meiji Shrine
  • Afternoon: Harajuku and Takeshita Street
  • Evening: Shinjuku nightlife district

Day 3: Tokyo to Kyoto

  • Morning: Shinkansen (bullet train) to Kyoto
  • Afternoon: Explore Arashiyama Bamboo Grove
  • Evening: Dinner in Pontocho Alley

Day 4: Kyoto - Temple Tour

  • Morning: Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)
  • Afternoon: Fushimi Inari Shrine and its thousand torii gates
  • Evening: Gion District, chance to spot Geisha

Day 5: Kyoto to Osaka

  • Morning: Train to Osaka
  • Afternoon: Osaka Castle
  • Evening: Dotonbori street food adventure

Day 6: Osaka - Food and Fun

  • Morning: Kuromon Ichiba Market food tour
  • Afternoon: Universal Studios Japan or Osaka Aquarium
  • Evening: Shinsekai area for local flavors

Day 7: Return to Tokyo and Departure

  • Morning: Shinkansen back to Tokyo
  • Afternoon: Last-minute shopping in Akihabara
  • Evening: Transfer to airport for departure

Travel Tips

Transportation

Consider purchasing a 7-day Japan Rail Pass before your trip if you plan to travel between cities. For local transportation, get a rechargeable IC card (Suica or Pasmo) which works on most public transit in major cities.

Etiquette

Remove shoes before entering homes, traditional ryokans, and some restaurants. Bow slightly when greeting people. Avoid eating while walking in public, and don't talk loudly on phones on public transport.

Money

Japan is still largely a cash-based society. Always carry cash with you as many smaller shops, restaurants, and attractions don't accept credit cards. ATMs that accept foreign cards can be found at 7-Eleven convenience stores.

Internet Access

For staying connected, consider renting a pocket WiFi device at the airport or pre-ordering one for delivery to your hotel. Many cafes and convenience stores also offer free WiFi.
